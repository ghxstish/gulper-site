Introduction

This policy explains what information Gulper collects, how it's used, and the choices you have. Gulper is a drink-tracking app that estimates your blood alcohol content (BAC) from what you log, so it necessarily handles some sensitive personal and health-related data — we've tried to keep this explanation as plain and specific as possible.

Information we collect

Account: your email address, password (never stored in plain text), first name, last name, username, and profile photo.

Personal details: date of birth, height, weight, and gender. These are used to estimate your BAC and to power your insights. They are not shown on your public profile, but some of them are shared with friends you accept — see "What other people can see" below.

Drink logs: the name, ingredients, volume, ABV, photo, and time of each drink you log. At the start of a night's drinking we also ask how full your stomach is, and store that answer against that night's drinks — how much you've eaten changes how alcohol is absorbed, so it's an input to your BAC estimate. Gulper does not ask for or record your location.

Photos for Scan Drink: a photo you take to identify a drink is sent to Anthropic, our AI provider, to analyze it. This only happens when you actively use Scan Drink, and your photos are only analyzed to answer that request — they are never used to train AI models.

Barcodes: when you scan a product barcode, that barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts, a public product database, to look up the product's name, size, and alcohol content.

Usage counts: we record the time of each drink and barcode scan you run, so we can enforce the daily scan limit on the free plan and prevent abuse of the AI scanning feature. These records count scans; they don't store what you scanned.

Social features: friend connections, group memberships, the people you block, and any report you make about another user (who you reported, the reason you chose, and when). Gulper has no messaging of any kind.

Subscription: your current plan (free or Gulper+), your billing cycle, and your renewal date. If you upgrade to Gulper+, the payment is handled entirely by Apple and charged to your Apple Account — Gulper never sees or stores your card number. RevenueCat, our subscription service, receives your Gulper account identifier and the purchase details from Apple so we know which account to unlock.

Preferences: your notification settings and your personalisation and measurement preferences, which are all off by default until you choose otherwise.

Notifications: if you allow notifications, we store your phone's push token so we can tell you when someone sends you a friend request, accepts yours, or adds you to a group. Reminders and warnings about your own drinking are scheduled on your phone and never pass through our servers.

Usage analytics: only if you allow it, when you finish signing up or later with Measure Content Performance in Profile → Manage Personalisation Preferences. It's off by default. Then we record which screens you open and when, when you open and leave the app, and some of the things you do: finishing sign-up, viewing the Gulper+ offer and buying or restoring it, sending or accepting a friend request, creating a group (and how many people are in it), and reporting or blocking someone (with the reason you chose). We also record whether you're on the free plan or Gulper+, and technical details such as your phone model, iOS version, app version, language, and time zone. This is linked to your account, but never includes what or how much you drink, your BAC, anyone's name, or anything you type. You can turn it off again at any time.

Information kept only on your device

Some things never leave your phone and are never sent to our servers: any drink scan you start while you're offline is held on your device until you reconnect, at which point it is uploaded as a normal drink log. Deleting the app removes anything still queued.

How we use your information

To estimate your BAC and show it back to you; to show your drink history, saved drinks, calendar, and statistics; to connect you with friends and groups you choose to interact with; to send you the notifications you've allowed; to identify drinks you photograph via Scan Drink and products you scan by barcode; to enforce plan limits and prevent abuse; to review reports about other users and act on them; to see which features get used so we can improve them, if you've allowed usage analytics; to process payments if you subscribe to Gulper+; and to respond if you contact support.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not serve third-party advertising in Gulper.

What other people can see

Your profile photo, first and last name, and username are visible to other users who can find you, and to members of any group you join.

When you accept a friend request, that friend can open your profile in the app and see your drinking information: your current estimated BAC and time until sober, your drinking calendar, and your insights and estimated tolerance. To calculate those figures their app also receives the details it needs to do the maths — your height, weight, gender, and date of birth, and the volume, alcohol content, time, and recorded stomach fullness of the drinks you've logged. It does not receive your drink names or drink photos.

Only people whose friend request you have accepted can see any of this. Removing or blocking a friend ends that access immediately. Someone you block can no longer send you friend requests or add you to a group, and isn't notified that you blocked them.

When you report someone, only the Gulper team sees the report. The person you reported isn't notified.

Group members can see each other’s names, usernames and photos, and the group’s own drinking statistics.

Third parties we use

Supabase hosts our database, authentication, and file storage. hCaptcha checks that it's a person, not a bot, signing up, signing in, or resetting a password, and receives technical information such as your IP address to do so. Anthropic analyzes photos submitted through Scan Drink. Open Food Facts is queried for product information when you scan a barcode. Expo's push service and Apple deliver the notifications you've allowed. PostHog, on servers in the EU, receives usage analytics only from people who've allowed it; it discards IP addresses and doesn't record location. Apple processes Gulper+ payments, and RevenueCat tells us which account a purchase belongs to so we can unlock it. None of these providers is permitted to use your data for anything beyond providing that specific service to Gulper.

Data retention and deletion

Your data is kept for as long as your account exists. You can permanently delete your account at any time from Profile → Account Actions → Delete Account. This removes your photos from storage and permanently deletes your profile, personal details, drink history, saved drinks, friendships, group memberships, blocks, and reports. It does not cancel a Gulper+ subscription: Apple bills it, so cancel it yourself in Settings → Apple Account → Subscriptions.

If you allowed usage analytics, deleting your account also deletes it from PostHog.

Your rights

You can access or correct most of your information directly in the app under Profile. You can request a copy of your data, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it by contacting us at supportgulper@gmail.com.

Age requirement

Gulper is not intended for anyone under the legal drinking age in their country, and is not directed at children. We don't knowingly collect information from children.

Security

Your data is protected by row-level access controls that restrict every database query to your own account — or, for the friend features described above, to accounts you've accepted as friends — and by encryption in transit. No method of storage or transmission is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your information using industry-standard practices.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update the date at the top of this page. Continued use of Gulper after a change means you accept the updated policy.
